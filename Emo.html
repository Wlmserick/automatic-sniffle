Designing for emotional awareness

Background

Emotional self-reflection can feel nagging and intrusive, making it difficult for users to log moods consistently or follow through on daily check-ins. At the same time, trying to track emotions without structure demands time and mental energy, especially for people already balancing stress, anxiety, and busy routines.

How might we help people make sense of their emotions,one day at a time?

Project Overview

EmoLog is a mobile application designed to help users log their moods, reflect on their emotional journey, and gain insights about their well-being over time. The core vision is to make emotional self-awareness simple, habit-building, and empowering.

Role: Solo UX Designer & Researcher

Timeline: 12 weeks (Research to Launch)

Tools: Figma, Notion, Miro

Problem Statement

Many people struggle to understand their emotional patterns. Daily stress, social pressures, and limited self-reflection make it difficult to recognize triggers or trends.

The Challenge: Design a tool that helps users effortlessly track emotions, reflect on feelings, and gain insights without overwhelming them.

Understanding emotional needs

Through interviews and surveys, I discovered key insights about how people relate to their emotions.

Research Methodology

12 user interviews with working professionals aged 25-45

Survey of 150+ participants about current mood tracking habits

Critical User Insights

73% abandon mood tracking apps within 2 weeks due to complexity

Users want context, not just mood ratings

Visual progress tracking motivates continued use

Privacy and data security are primary concerns

From insights to interface

Iterative design process focused on simplicity and meaningful reflection.

Key Learnings

Simplicity wins: Reducing cognitive load increased engagement by 40%

Context matters: Users valued reflection prompts over just mood ratings

Visual feedback drives habit: Progress visualization improved retention

Privacy first: Clear data practices built user trust
